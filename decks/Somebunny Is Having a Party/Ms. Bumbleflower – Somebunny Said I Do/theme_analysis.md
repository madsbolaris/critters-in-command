# Theme Analysis — Somebunny Couldn't Say No

> Every card in the **Ms. Bumbleflower** Bracket 4 build, scored for fit with **"Somebunny Couldn't Say No"** — a Bant (G/W/U) forced-draw *mill* deck built around a runaway **destination wedding**. One True Love's Kiss and Ms. Bumbleflower has found the one, with a single demand: **everybunny must be there** when she says "I do." Not at one ceremony — she books the *whole world*, and the guests are coming along for every leg. Each new stop (a land) means another flight, another hotel, another gift, and she *insists* nobody skip a single chapel on the tour. Every card an opponent draws is another bill charged to their account; every toll and tithe she collects at the door bankrolls the next upgrade. A library is a bank account, and when a guest's savings finally run dry they're **bankrupt** — decked out, out of the celebration, and out of the game. The more of the globe she books, the faster the whole table goes broke. *Till deck do us part.*

### Deck Column Key
- **B4** — in the Bracket 4 build (the only build for this host so far)

### Scoring Guide
- **10**: Perfect — the bride, her wedding tour, or the core "bankrupt the guests" engine
- **7–9**: Strong — clearly on-theme (wedding / guest / itinerary / destination flavor + good synergy)
- **4–6**: Moderate — a functional part of the celebration
- **1–3**: Weak — pure power/utility card, minimal wedding flavor

### Key Takeaways

- **The win condition *is* the story.** Ms. Bumbleflower's around-the-world wedding is so extravagant it bankrupts the guests. A library is a bank account; every card an opponent draws is another bill; when the account hits zero they're broke (decked out) and eliminated. Forced draw and mill are the same invoice.
- **Every land is a destination — and more stops mean faster bankruptcy.** The world tour is all about *scale*, so the deck leans into extra land drops: Exploration, Burgeoning, Tempt with Discovery, and Rites of Flourishing keep booking venues, turning a flood of lands into mana for the billing engines and thinning your deck so the invoices (your draws) keep flowing. Kruphix and Reliquary Tower bank the surplus.
- **The billing engines are the payoff.** Psychic Corrosion, both Tutelages, Forced Fruition, and Riverchurn Monument charge the guests directly; Bruvac and the doublers (Teferi's Ageless Insight, Alhammarret's Archive) charge them twice.
- **Opens like a group hug.** Teferi's Puzzle Box, Temple Bell, Font of Mythos, Rites of Flourishing, Dictate of Kruphix, and Kwain make *everyone* draw (spend) — so early on you look like the harmless bride nobody wants to attack, right up until the payoffs turn all that spending fatal.
- **You never go broke.** Elixir of Immortality and Gaea's Blessing reshuffle your own graveyard, and your card advantage (Rhystic Study, Sylvan Library, Consecrated Sphinx) keeps the bride flush while the guests drain — the two-sided economy that wins the long game.
- **Two Rabbits, plus tokens, with a Bloomburrow accent.** Ms. Bumbleflower and Kwain are literal Rabbits, and a scattering of Bloomburrow art keeps the woodland accent.
- **Bracket 4 power, honestly kept.** Sol Ring, Ancient Tomb, the ABUR duals, fetches, Rhystic Study, Cyclonic Rift, Teferi's Protection, and Smothering Tithe hold the power level without abandoning the wedding.
- **Repeatable mill artifacts close the deal.** Riverchurn Monument taps to mill any players two at a time (and never the host), and its exhaust mode sweeps away their swollen graveyards.
- **Shared printings match Baylen.** Every card that also appears in the Baylen deck now uses the same artwork/printing across both decks (Sol Ring, Swords to Plowshares, Command Tower, the basics, etc.).
- **Self-preservation is the pillow fort.** Reliquary Tower, Elixir of Immortality, Glacial Chasm, Sphere of Safety, and The One Ring keep the host safe and un-decked while the guests draw themselves out.
