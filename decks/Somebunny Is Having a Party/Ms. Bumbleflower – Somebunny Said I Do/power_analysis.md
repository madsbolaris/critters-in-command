# Power Analysis — Somebunny Couldn't Say No (Ms. Bumbleflower, B4)

> Card-by-card evaluation of **raw power and function** (independent of flavor), plus a cohesion verdict. Companion to [theme_analysis.md](theme_analysis.md), which scores the same 100 cards for flavor fit.

### Power Scale
- **9–10**: Format-defining / premium staple — always great.
- **7–8**: Strong — high impact in this deck's plan.
- **5–6**: Solid role-player — pulls its weight, replaceable.
- **3–4**: Filler / situational — a cut candidate on an upgrade.
- **1–2**: Weak — actively holding the deck back.

### Role Key
Ramp · Fix · Enabler (makes everyone draw) · Payoff (turns draws/mill into a win) · Mult (mill multiplier) · Draw (your card advantage) · Removal · Counter · Protect · Pillow (attack tax / damage prevention) · Tutor · Recur · Alt-win · Land

## Mill payoffs — the wincon core (10)

> The deck wins by decking opponents out. These convert your (and the table's) heavy drawing into library loss.

| Card | Role | Power | Note |
|------|------|:-----:|------|
| Psychic Corrosion | Payoff | 8 | The core: *every* card you draw mills each opponent 2. In a deck this draw-dense, it shreds the whole table. |
| Sphinx's Tutelage | Payoff | 7 | Same trigger, single target, with a color-match chain that can run away. |
| Teferi's Tutelage | Payoff | 6 | Third copy of the plan; two per draw, single target. |
| Bruvac the Grandiloquent | Mult | 7 | Doubles **all** your mill — a true force-multiplier. Dead without another mill source, so it's a stacker not a standalone. |
| Fraying Sanity | Mult | 6 | A curse on one opponent — every card they lose that turn, they lose *twice*, so a single Psychic Corrosion trigger empties their checkbook at double speed. A single-target Bruvac; stack the two and a rival is bankrupt in a turn. Dead without another mill source. |
| Riverchurn Monument | Payoff / Removal | 6 | Repeatable one-sided mill 2 at will, plus an exhaust mode that sweeps their swollen graveyards. Never hits you. |
| Maddening Cacophony | Payoff (burst) | 6 | Mill 8, or kicked to mill half each opponent's library. Big, one-shot spike. |
| Traumatize | Payoff (burst) | 6 | Mills the target's whole top half at once — a huge one-shot spike, and with Bruvac or Fraying Sanity on them it decks them outright. Single-target and sorcery-speed, so it's a finisher, not an engine. |
| Forced Fruition | Payoff | 6 | Opponent casts a spell → they draw seven (huge with Psychic Corrosion). But it also *digs them to answers/combo* — high-variance, double-edged. |
| Windfall | Draw / Payoff | 6 | Refills your hand and triggers every "on draw" payoff at once; symmetric wheel. |

**Assessment:** enough redundancy that one sweep doesn't turn the deck off, and Bruvac makes any single payoff lethal. The real question isn't *power* here — it's *speed* (see verdict): mill is a slow clock in a 4-player pod, and several of these hand opponents cards too.

## Group-draw enablers — feed the payoffs & deck opponents (7)

| Card | Role | Power | Note |
|------|------|:-----:|------|
| Teferi's Puzzle Box | Enabler | 7 | Every opponent redraws a whole fresh hand each turn — a torrent of forced draws, and with a mill payoff online each redraw shaves a stack off the top. One of the strongest "everyone draws" pieces you run. |
| Font of Mythos | Enabler | 6 | Everyone draws **two** extra per turn — enormous mill fuel, but the fastest way to arm your opponents too. |
| Generous Patron | Enabler / Draw | 5 | A flexible {2}{G} body: the ETB either pins two +1/+1 counters on an opponent's creature or throws the floor open for a table-wide draw, and its static draws you a card whenever you put counters on a creature you don't control. Group-hug on demand that can quietly flip into a private draw engine feeding the mill. |
| Selvala, Explorer Returned | Enabler / Ramp | 6 | Parley draws the whole table and pays you mana + life per nonland flipped — group draw and ramp on one tap. |
| Temple Bell | Enabler / Combo | 6 | Tap: everyone draws — and you control the timing. Also the untap target that goes infinite with Mind Over Matter. |
| Wedding Ring | Enabler / Draw | 6 | You draw whenever a gifted opponent draws or gains life — a parity engine that feeds your mill (and half of a combo with Consecrated Sphinx). |
| Jace, Wielder of Mysteries | Payoff / Insurance | 8 | Anti-deck-out static (draw from empty library = win) turns your own group-draw into a fail-safe; +1 mills an opponent **and** draws you a card, and the −8 is a self-mill win. |

**Assessment:** the "group-hug" opening that makes you look harmless — and every extra draw is mill fuel (via your own draws) or a punisher trigger (Consecrated Sphinx, Trouble in Pairs). The tension: **Font of Mythos, Teferi's Puzzle Box, and Forced Fruition also dig your opponents toward answers or a combo.** Against a fast combo deck, your generosity can backfire — which is why the interaction suite below matters.

Crucially, the private engines (Rhystic, Sphinx, Sylvan, The One Ring) advance the mill *without* arming opponents, unlike the symmetric group-draw. The flip side is self-inflicted library pressure (see verdict).

|------|------|:-----:|------|

**Assessment:** the interaction upgrade is the point of the latest tune. Unlike mono-GW, blue makes this a real strength rather than a hole.

| Card | Role | Power | Note |

| Hall of Heliod's Generosity | Recur / Land | 6 | A land that keeps buying back your best enchantment (Psychic Corrosion, a Tutelage, Sphere of Safety) from the graveyard, one per turn — grindy insurance that the engine never truly dies. |

**Assessment:** the tutor suite is now both deeper and broader — Enlightened + Idyllic find the artifact/enchantment engine, while **Long-Term Plans** (any card) and **Mystical Tutor** (any instant/sorcery) finally reach the win conditions and combo pieces the deck could never search for, directly addressing the lowest CRISPI stat (Consistency). **Replenish + Hall of Heliod's Generosity** give the enchantment core real resilience against wipes, and **Elixir of Immortality** answers the self-deck risk.

## Combo finishes & the fast kill

The mill plan has a combo gear that ends the game outright rather than grinding:

| Line | Power | Note |
|------|:-----:|------|
| Mind Over Matter + Temple Bell / Folio of Fancies | 8 | Discard cards to untap the Bell (or channel Folio) and draw the whole table's decks away in one turn — with a mill payoff online it's lethal. Mind Over Matter is the deck's true "press to win" gear. |
| Bruvac + Maddening Cacophony (kicked) | 7 | Kicked Cacophony mills half each library; Bruvac doubles it to the *whole* library — a one-card table wipe of decks. |
| Consecrated Sphinx + Wedding Ring / forced draw | 6 | Sphinx plus a forced-draw engine buries opponents while burying *you* in cards to convert through the mill. |

**Assessment:** the deck is not purely a grind — Mind Over Matter turns any draw engine into an immediate deck-out, and Bruvac + a burst mill is a fast backup. These lines are why the "slow" mill plan can close abruptly; protecting them is what the new counter suite is for.

## Manabase (39 lands)

Three-color Bant fixing: **Command Tower / City of Brass** (any color), **Tundra / Tropical Island / Savannah** (ABUR duals), the shock/check/fast lands (**Hallowed Fountain, Breeding Pool, Temple Garden, Glacial Fortress, Hinterland Harbor, Sunpetal Grove, Botanical Sanctum, Seachrome Coast, Razorverge Thicket**), untapped **pain lands for speed (Adarkar Wastes, Brushland, Yavimaya Coast)** and **Horizon Canopy** (which also sacs for a card), painless filters & pathways (**Mystic Gate, Flooded Grove, Hengegate Pathway**), **4 fetches** (Flooded Strand, Windswept Heath, Misty Rainforest, Prismatic Vista), ramp land **Ancient Tomb**, and utility: **Serra's Sanctum** (a flood of white off the ~15 enchantments), **Mikokoro** (group draw), **Reliquary Tower** (no max hand size), **Hall of Heliod's Generosity** (enchantment recursion), the pillow lands **Glacial Chasm / Maze of Ith**, and channel lands **Boseiju / Otawara**. Just **5 basics** (3 Island, Plains, Forest) behind 4 fetches.

**Assessment:** 39 lands + ~9 accelerants is **mana-heavy** for a 2–7 curve; expect to flood, which the mana sinks absorb. Fixing is excellent, and Serra's Sanctum can produce absurd mana in the enchantment-dense midgame. The thin basic count is the one real fragility — a Blood Moon or Back to Basics bites harder than the budget suggests.

## Cohesion Verdict

**Overall power: high (strong Bracket 4).** A focused, redundant Bant mill-control deck: ramp out → deploy an "everyone draws" engine → turn that generosity lethal with Psychic Corrosion / the Tutelages (doubled by Bruvac) → survive behind a deep pillow fort and, now, a real counter suite until the table decks out or Mind Over Matter ends it outright.

### Strengths
- **A real, redundant wincon engine.** Multiple "on-draw" mill payoffs + Bruvac as a multiplier, fed by one of the deepest draw suites you'll see. One sweep doesn't turn it off — and **Replenish + Hall of Heliod's Generosity** rebuild the enchantments if it does.
- **Interaction most mill decks can't run.** Blue gives you Mana Drain, Counterspell, Force of Negation, Swan Song, An Offer, plus Teferi, Time Raveler and Cyclonic Rift — the tools to survive the combo decks that race mill, and to protect the fragile engine (the exact hole the deck used to have).
- **Deep pillow fort.** Teferi's Protection, Glacial Chasm, Sphere of Safety, Maze of Ith, plus lifelink walls make you very hard to kill in combat.
- **A fast backdoor.** Mind Over Matter + Temple Bell/Folio, or Bruvac + a burst mill, can end the game in one turn — the "slow" deck isn't only slow.
- **Elite card advantage + consistency.** Rhystic, Consecrated Sphinx, Sylvan, Mangara, The One Ring, Folio + two enchantment tutors — you rarely run dry.

### Weaknesses / risks
1. **You mill yourself too.** You draw *harder than anyone*, but Elixir of Immortality reshuffles your graveyard to de-risk it. Still the axis to watch.
2. **Symmetric generosity arms opponents.** Font of Mythos, Teferi's Puzzle Box, and Forced Fruition dig opponents toward answers/combo — a genuine cost of the plan, now partly offset by the counter suite.
3. **Slow clock without the combo.** Mill is slow in a 4-player pod; you often need Bruvac + a payoff, or Mind Over Matter, online.
4. **Over-ramped / flood-prone.** ~9 accelerants + 39 lands for a low-to-mid curve; mitigated by mana sinks, not eliminated.
5. **Thin basics.** Just 5 basics behind 4 fetches — a Blood Moon or Back to Basics bites harder than the budget suggests.

### Suggested upgrade slots (rough priority)
1. **A fourth on-draw mill payoff** to lean harder into the primary wincon.
2. **More tutor depth** — Long-Term Plans + Mystical Tutor now reach the combos and win conditions; a third universal tutor would further stabilize Consistency, the lowest CRISPI stat.
3. **Trim ramp if you flood** — a redundant rock or a fetch is the first cut before touching fixing.
4. **Shore up the basics** (an extra basic or two) against nonbasic hate.

**Bottom line:** mechanically cohesive and powerful — a rare *interactive* mill deck with a redundant engine, a fast combo backdoor, and a deep survival shell. The recent tune closed its biggest hole (stack interaction to protect the engine); its remaining risks — self-decking, clock speed, thin basics — are inherent to the archetype, not build errors.
